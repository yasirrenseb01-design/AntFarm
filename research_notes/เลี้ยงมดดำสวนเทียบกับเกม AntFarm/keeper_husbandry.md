## Founding: setup, catching, disturbance, feeding, first brood, failure

### Takeaway
Everyone agrees on a dark, undisturbed, water-backed test tube. The real split is feeding: sheets say never feed a claustral queen, while a 2024 experiment found fed queens raised more brood. First workers arrive in about 4-6 weeks. No usable keeper-reported failure rate or first-brood count was found.

### Cited Findings
**Catching**
- Flights run June to September, with European ants mating in July and August; queens can be found by gently flipping stones. [vendor, advice, F] — [Antkeepers care sheet](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger)
- Real catch dates: 14 July 2018 in the northern Netherlands, 27 queens from one flight [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier journal](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier); 21 July 2013, location unstated [single, observed, F] — [Pierre's report](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/8406-pierre-s-lasius-niger/)

**Tube and disturbance**
- Tube about one third water, cotton plug, kept dark. [vendor, advice, S] — [search results for care sheets, including AntsDavey and ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/product/lasius-niger/) (results set)
- Quiet, dark spot at room temperature, away from vibration, disturbed as little as possible. If water runs low or mould appears, connect a fresh tube rather than handling her. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- No numeric disturbance limit (such as checks per week) was found in any source.

**Feeding a claustral queen: disagreement**
- Do not feed: she lives on reserves, and the tube's water is enough. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Opening the tube to feed stresses her and risks mould. [vendor, advice, S] — [care-sheet search results](https://antontop.com/product/lasius-niger/) (results set)
- Against that: in a study of 240 field-collected queens, regularly fed queens raised more pupae and workers, and the authors suggest crickets for labs wanting a bigger first generation. They also flag feeding's possible risks and stress. [peer, observed, S; group means not obtained] — [Szabó, Nagy and Tartally, Biol J Linn Soc 2024, repository link](https://dea.lib.unideb.hu/items/0c91c0b0-5811-4cde-a127-12d8a690c8fa/full)

**Time to first workers and brood size**
- First workers in about 4-6 weeks. [vendor, advice, S] — [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/product/lasius-niger/)
- Measured by one keeper: tubes set up 20 July 2018, first nanitics 22 August, about 33 days (about 39 from the flight). [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Egg to adult totals about 1-1.5 months, sometimes up to 2: egg 10-15 days, larva 10-15 days, pupa 10-25 days. [species database, advice-level summary, F; not independently verified] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/)
- The only first-brood number found: 12 nanitics at the first update, which is not a final clutch count. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)

**Failure**
- 27 of 27 queens alive after the first hibernation (March 2019). The figure starts from the 27 listed at founding and is one keeper in one year. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Wild founding: over 95% of queens are estimated to die. This cites earlier work and describes field conditions, and the excerpt may refer to ants generally. It is not a captive rate. [peer, S] — [Tragust et al. 2020](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/text)
- All queens started a colony in all four microbial environments, and soil housing gave more workers than sterile housing. Queens in pathogen-treated soil had lower survival after hibernation. [peer, observed, F, abstract only] — [Tragust et al. 2020](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/text)
- Queens may co-found (up to 25% of colonies listed as pleometrotic) [database, F] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/); after first workers emerge they fight until one remains [peer, S] — [founding search results, including AntWiki](https://antwiki.org/wiki/Colony_Foundation) (results set)

### Inferences
- Captive founding failure is probably far below the wild figure, because predation and exposure are removed, but one 27-of-27 result cannot set a rate.
- "Never feed" and the Szabó result are not strictly contradictory: sheets are managing beginner risk (mould, stress), while the study measures brood output under controlled feeding.

### Gaps
- Keeper-reported queen failure percentage. None found.
- First clutch and first-brood size, measured. The Szabó group means would fill this, but the full text could not be opened.
- Effect of temperature on time to nanitics in keeper logs.

## Temperature and humidity

### Takeaway
Keepers cluster around 20-25 C for the nest, and room temperature is called sufficient. Heat is optional, offered as a local warm spot rather than whole-nest heating. Published humidity ranges disagree widely, and the practical advice is to give a choice of wet and dry and avoid leaks.

### Cited Findings
**Nest temperature by source (active season)**

| Source | Nest C | Outworld C | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet) | 20-25 | 18-28 | vendor, F |
| [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/) | 21-24 | 18-28 | database, F |
| [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger) | 20-27 | not given | vendor, F |
| [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/product/lasius-niger/) | 22-28 | 22-28 | vendor, S |
| A UK-breeder PDF, one of [this](https://www.antantics.wales/_files/ugd/54884f_e1d578da55d2451aa7b984c76122c124.pdf) or [this](https://www.antsrus.com/_files/ugd/d392ea_f0edbafd07c34fb697e27f6bf790d094.pdf) | 18-22 | 18-25 | vendor, S, exact PDF unconfirmed |

- Keeper practice: aims for 21-25 C; in a 34 C heatwave tried to hold the ant room under 30 C. [single, Netherlands, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.3](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-3)
- A forum claim that brood needs 20 C or higher to develop properly. [single, S] — [antstore.net forum](https://www.antstore.net/forum/topic13013.html) (results set)

**Heat mats and gradients**
- Optional in summer: a hot spot for cocoons speeds development. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- If heating, about 25 C for a couple of hours a day, on one part of the setup only. [vendor, advice, F] — [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger)
- A keeper reports the ants disliked a heat cable laid over the nest glass. [single, observed, F] — [Felix's journal](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19740-felixs-epic-lasius-niger/)

**Humidity: disagreement**
- Nest 40-60%, outworld 30-70%, species not fussy. [vendor, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Nest 50-60%, arena 30-50%. [database, F] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/)
- "10-50%" with light misting, an outlier low range. [vendor, F] — [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger)

**Managing moisture, by setup**
- Test tube: the water side ran dry after about 11 weeks in one log (20 July to 3 October 2018), which forced a move to a new tube. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier). A UK keeper faced the same dry tube just before hibernation. [single, observed, F] — [Formiculture hibernation thread](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/)
- 3D-printed nest: seemed too wet, so the keeper added a second, dry nest, and most of the colony moved within days. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Ytong: a reservoir's glue seal failed, and water poured in instead of diffusing. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Soil and sand: workers dug in over-wet sand but did not move brood into it. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.3](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-3)
- Leaks can be catastrophic: a leaking tank flooded one colony, leaving about 25 workers. [single, observed, F] — [Pierre's report](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/8406-pierre-s-lasius-niger/)
- Oversized, too-damp nests are linked to waste-related mould and mites. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)

### Inferences
- A wet-side and dry-side choice, which the colony resolves by moving, fits keeper reports better than a single target percentage. The keeper logs report colony responses, not hygrometer readings.

### Gaps
- Stage-specific temperature ranges (founding vs. larvae vs. pupae) were not stated as numbers beyond the cocoon hot-spot advice.
- Described visual signs of "too dry" in the species, beyond empty water sides and colonies relocating.
- Plaster and acrylic moisture practice specific to L. niger: nothing found.

## Feeding

### Takeaway
Advice is qualitative and scaled by eye: constant sugar and protein a few times a week, with leftovers removed in about 3 days. No source measured intake, so the requested figures for 10, 100 and 1000 workers do not exist in what I could read.

### Cited Findings
- 1-20 workers: weekly carbohydrate drop no larger than the queen's head; three days later about 1/4 of a gut-loaded mealworm, or chicken or ham no bigger than a pinky nail; remove protein after three days and add another sugar drop. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Adult colony: food and water available at all times; freshly killed feeders; sugar water, honey water or commercial nectar. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Portion ladder: half a mealworm or "1 fly leg" per feed at 1-15 workers, one mealworm or a cricket leg at 15-50. Unverified, and the mix of sizes looks unreliable. [vendor, advice, S] — [ANTonTOP beginners page](https://antontop.com/?p=22079) (results set)
- Protein 2-3 times a week with constant sugar. [care guide, advice, S] — [antscout](https://antscout.web.app/species/lasius-niger) (results set)
- Replace sugar solution about every 3 days, because humidity dilutes it and it ferments. [guide, advice, S] — [antcube](https://antcube.shop/?p=5380) (results set)
- Food goes in the arena, never in nest chambers. [guide, advice, S] — [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/?p=22079) (results set)
- Real menu and frequency: August 2018 tiny honey drops and fruit flies, then feeders about every 2 days; by July 2019 fed daily, mainly fruit flies, mealworms, small spiders, crickets. Occasional cooked egg, ham, raw chicken. Sugars: sugar water, honey, maple syrup, acacia honey. Sugar water in tubes tended to leak after a few days. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Carcasses built up: about 1.5 months' worth removed in one cleaning of a large colony's outworld. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Water: the tube's reservoir early on, and always available even in hibernation. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- L. niger generally prefers higher sugar concentrations. [peer, S] — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-56133-y)
- A student presentation tested Lasius feeding at twice weekly, weekly and fortnightly, concluding only that some regimes are better. [non-peer abstract, S, Lasius species unconfirmed] — [Butler University](https://digitalcommons.butler.edu/urc/2018/biology/18)

### Inferences
- A rule that matches keeper practice: sugar nearly always present, and protein cadence rising from weekly at under 20 workers to several times a week or daily as larvae pile up. This is a synthesis, not a stated standard.

### Gaps
- Grams or millilitres consumed by colonies of 10, 100 or 1000 workers: none found. A Dryad dataset on [ant colony macronutrient intake](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.g79cnp5n1) surfaced and might help, but it was not opened, and its species was not confirmed.

## Hibernation

### Takeaway
Practice centres on late October to March (about 4 months) at about 5-8 C in a fridge or unheated room, with a ramp of about a week. Science supports avoiding the 2 C end for colonies with workers. Care sheets treat skipping one first winter as survivable and repeated skipping as harmful, though no source measured that harm.

### Cited Findings
**Dates and duration**
- End of October to end of March. [vendor, UK, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- October to about March. [vendor, advice, F] — [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger); Oct-March [database, S] — [ameisenhaltung.de PDF entry](https://ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry-pdf-export/226-lasius-niger)
- Late October to early March. [vendor, S] — [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/pl/?p=103263)
- **Disagreement on length:** "3-4 months" (Virginia, USA) vs. "a solid four" with three called too short. [single each, advice, F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/). Another forum poster says at least 4-6 weeks. [single, S] — [antstore.net](https://www.antstore.net/forum/topic4837.html) (results set)

**Actual keeper records**

| Keeper | Climate | In | Out | Temp C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Wegmier 2018-19](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier) | N. Netherlands | about 24 Nov | 12 Mar | 5-7 fridge |
| [Wegmier 2020](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4) | N. Netherlands | about 20 Oct | not read | fridge, dropped to 0.5 by mistake |

(all [single, observed, F]; the UK row is a plan, not an outcome)

**Temperature**
- 5-10 C, and an unheated room works if under 15 C; keep above 3 C. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- 5-10 C for 4 months "mandatory" [vendor, S] — [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/product/lasius-niger/); 6 C [vendor, S] — [Formica Market](https://formicamarket.com/hi/species/lasius-niger); about 10 C and no lower [vendor, S, AntKit] — [care-sheet search results](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1031/4421/files/Lasius-niger.pdf) (results set, exact page unconfirmed); 2-10 C [single, F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/); 6-8 C [single, S] — [antstore.net](https://www.antstore.net/forum/topic4837.html) (results set)
- **Measured:** 280 mated queens (Turku, Finland, 2009 and 2010) plus 1-year-old colonies, kept at +7-8 C vs. +2 C. Workers survived worse at +2 C. Queen survival varied by year but not by temperature, and queens at +2 C kept more body fat. [peer, observed, S, abstract only; percentages not obtained] — [Haatanen, van Ooik and Sorvari 2015](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3223334?lang=en_US)

**Fridge vs. cellar**
- Fridge preferred for steadier temperature than a shed. [single, Virginia, advice, F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/)
- Garage or shed is fine with polystyrene insulation and no frost. [vendor, UK, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)

**Ramp and wake-up**
- Down: coolest room for about a week, then garage or shed. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet). Keeper version: heating off and windows open [single, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier); or gradually lowered room temperature through mid-October [single, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Up: 12 C, then 16 C, then 21 C, over about a week. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)

**Food and water in winter: disagreement**
- No feeding [vendor, F] — [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/pages/care-sheet-lasius-niger)
- Water always, optional small food every other month [vendor, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Small outworld with optional sugar water inside the fridge [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)

**Brood**
- Colony went into a fridge with "thousands of larvae"; six weeks in, workers had stacked larvae into piles 1.5-2 cm high, and dead workers and dead larvae were dumped in the outworld. The keeper judged the 0.5 C accident harmless, though no count of deaths was made. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Larvae in tubes did not pupate until winter passed (October 2021 to March 2022). This followed a colony crisis, so it may not be typical. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.6](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-6)
- One keeper expected the last 15-20 pupae to emerge before cooling. [single, F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/)

**Losses**
- First winter: all 27 queens alive, only two dead workers found. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- A colony of about 1,000 workers suffered a "massive die off" over hibernation 2022-23, cause not established. [single, observed, F] — [Felix](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19740-felixs-epic-lasius-niger/)

**Skipping and first-year practice**
- First hibernation can be skipped, not as routine, and do it the next year. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Skipping does not kill quickly, but repeated years wear out the queen and shorten her life. [vendor, advice, S; no data given] — [ANTonTOP](https://antontop.com/pl/?p=103263)
- A lone queen can simply go in the fridge. [single, S] — [antstore.net](https://www.antstore.net/forum/post36789.html) (results set)
- In practice, colonies of 30-70 workers were hibernated in their first autumn. [single each, observed, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier); [UK keeper](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/)

### Inferences
- Start date follows outdoor climate and the keeper's room, not the calendar: the same keeper entered on 24 November one year and 20 October another.
- Fridges tested at 3-4 C (UK thread) sit closer to the +2 C treatment that hurt workers than to the +7-8 C one, so the common "fridge" advice may carry a real cost for worker-bearing colonies. This is my reading; the study did not test 3-4 C.

### Gaps
- How keepers check on colonies during winter (frequency, method): nothing specific found beyond one keeper's December inspection.
- Measured effect of skipping hibernation on L. niger: nothing found, only care-sheet assertion.
- What happens to eggs in winter: not stated in anything read.

## Enclosures and moving

### Takeaway
The usual path is tube, then tube-in-tub, then a real nest at about 50-100 workers. Keepers warn against oversizing. Moves rely on drying or lighting the old nest and offering a dark, moist new one, and can take days to weeks. Heat is contested for Lasius. Fluon is the default barrier but fails in humid enclosures.

### Cited Findings
**Upgrade timing**
- 1-20 workers: large tube. 20-50: add a foraging area. 50-100: Ytong, acrylic, 3D-printed or bioactive nest, plus outworld. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Observed: tubes for about 11 months, then a 3D nest on 9 June 2019 (about 80-90 workers in April). [single, F] — [Wegmier](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Observed: at almost 200 workers, a 20 x 10 basin with tube felt too small; planned a 30 x 20 x 20 arena with a 25 x 20 Ytong block. Units are not stated, presumably cm. [single, F] — [Pierre](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/8406-pierre-s-lasius-niger/)

**Formicarium types**
- A large colony ran several Ytong nests, including a vertical one, with satellites connected via the outworld. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Another keeper's two handmade Ytong nests both had cracked glass by May 2023, about 2 years after the colony was caught; the nests' own age is not stated. [single, observed, F] — [Felix](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19740-felixs-epic-lasius-niger/)
- Sand-loam suits the arena. [database, F] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/)

**Oversized nests**
- Too large causes stress and waste-related mould or mites. Starting in tub-and-tube is recommended. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)

**Making a colony move**
- Old nest: heat off, bright light. New nest: warm, fully dark. [forum, advice, S] — [Formiculture moving threads](https://formiculture.com/topic/1166-how-to-move-your-ants) (results set)
- **Disagreement on heat:** heating the new tube moves brood in minutes, vs. an experienced keeper saying several Lasius species avoid moderate heat while Camponotus follows it. Overheating can kill brood. [forum, single each, S; not L. niger-specific] — [results set](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/10805-how-to-move-ants-to-a-new-tube/)
- Foil over the new tube and non-heating light on the old; or tip and tap workers out; or let the old tube dry so they seek moisture. Moves can take weeks. [forum, advice, S] — [results set](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/21071-herding-ants-ways-to-get-ants-to-move-nest/)
- L. niger observed: drying old nests was used to encourage moving; a moist/dry nest pair drew most of the colony in days; about 5 workers died at one nest connection. [single, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4); [Wegmier p.1](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier)
- Larger colonies are easier to move, a moderator says, because there are more workers to complete it. [single, advice, F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19202-lasius-niger-hibernation/)

**Escape barriers**
- PTFE or branded barrier at the top edge. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Paraffin oil frame planned by a German keeper. [single, F] — [Pierre](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/8406-pierre-s-lasius-niger/)
- PTFE fails quickly and irreversibly in humid, poorly ventilated lidded boxes, and needs a smooth, clean surface. [vendor-affiliated keeper, S] — [Formiculture Fluon threads](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/21696-why-alternatives-to-fluon/) (results set)
- Paraffin: one keeper prefers it where Fluon failed, others say bigger colonies bypass oil barriers. [single each, S] — [Formiculture](https://formiculture.com/topic/12097-fluon-alternatives) (results set)
- Talc-ethanol lasted over three months and was stable for one year, in a small-species test, not L. niger. [journal blog, S] — [Myrmecological News blog](https://blog.myrmecologicalnews.org/2019/04/10/talc-powder-ethanol-a-simple-and-efficient-method-for-preventing-ants-from-escaping/)
- Real failures: 1,000-2,000 workers (keeper's guess) passed a talc barrier in April 2021, about 200 returned [single, F] — [Wegmier p.6](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-6); in September 2020, workers chewed through a wet cotton plug, about 100 recovered [single, F] — [Wegmier p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Large colonies need "considerable" escape prevention. [database, F] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/artenverzeichnis/entry/226-niger/)

### Inferences
- Moving methods recur in general ant forums, but L. niger-specific evidence favours moisture contrast, and that is also the lowest-risk cue. Heat should be treated as unproven for this species.

### Gaps
- Nest-to-outworld size ratios as a rule: only Pierre's one planned setup.
- Keeper comparison of plaster, acrylic and soil setups for L. niger: not found in readable sources.
- Effects of a cramped nest beyond a keeper's feeling that it had become too small.

## Colony growth as keepers report it

### Takeaway
Real logs give about 40-70 workers at the end of season 1, a sharp jump in seasons 2-3, and thousands by about two years in. Almost none of this is counted, the higher figures are guesses, and the headline growth tables are estimates. The largest log was also boosted with brood from other colonies, so it overstates a single queen's output.

**A captive colony can crash.** In the same log: after June 2021 alates were culled, larvae stopped pupating for about two months and workers were not replaced, and the keeper says he does not know why. Changes to humidity, temperature and food had no effect. His theory of a disrupted biological clock from early alates is unconfirmed, as are the members' ideas. The nests were put in a freezer in July 2021 and the queen and 30 workers moved to a tube. The summary I read leaves unclear how much of the fall from "20,000" to 30 was the stall versus the keeper's own freezing. Recovery: about 30 in March 2022, 200+ in July, 300+ in August 2022 with the 1,000 mark expected by hibernation. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.6](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-6); [p.7](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-7)

### Inferences
- Acceleration is reported around season 2 to 3: a colony sat near 80-90 in April of season 2 and reached 2,000-3,000 by July of season 3, about 15 months later.
- Pierre's 200 versus Wegmier's thousands at similar ages shows how much early setbacks and feeding and brood boosting can matter. Year-based tables hide that spread.
- Worker numbers above a few hundred in these logs are visual guesses, so they may run high or low.

### Gaps
- A counted (not estimated) L. niger worker series beyond the first season: none found.
- Year 4 and 5 data from a colony not boosted with foreign brood: none found.
- Wegmier's season 2 count (unread pages 2 and 5).

## Daily routine

### Takeaway
Little direct evidence. No keeper I read stated what they find most satisfying or tedious. Routine can only be reconstructed from care sheets and logged chores.

### Cited Findings
- Small colony: feed sugar, then protein three days later, then clear leftovers and add sugar, about weekly. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Checks: watch for mould, and top up or replace dry water sources. [vendor, advice, F] — [AntsDavey](https://www.antsdavey.co.uk/lasiusniger-caresheet)
- Logged chores in a large colony: daily feeding, periodic outworld cleaning, building new nests each season, repairing seals, and fixing escapes. A relative handled feeding once in a one-week absence. [single, observed, F] — [Wegmier p.1](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier), [p.3](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-3), [p.4](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-4)
- Maintenance of glass and Ytong nests: cracked panes needing replacement. [single, observed, F] — [Felix](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19740-felixs-epic-lasius-niger/)
- Closest thing to a statement of satisfaction: a keeper describing larvae covering the nest floor as the colony "developing nicely." [single, F] — [Wegmier p.7](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-7)

### Inferences
- Chore load appears to rise sharply with colony size, moving from a weekly drop-and-piece routine to daily feeding and nest construction. Escape risk grows too, because the largest losses were at large colony size.
- In the founding phase the correct routine is close to doing nothing, which is a notable fact when comparing against a game.

### Gaps
- Direct keeper statements on what is satisfying or tedious, and a stated weekly routine by a real keeper. These likely sit in r/antkeeping and YouTube, which I could not read.
