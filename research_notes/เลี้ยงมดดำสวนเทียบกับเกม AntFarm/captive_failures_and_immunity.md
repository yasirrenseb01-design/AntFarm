# What goes wrong when keeping Lasius niger in captivity, and what colonies do about it

Tags: [Ln] measured in L. niger; [relative] other species; [keeper] forum or care sheet; [inference] my reasoning.

How each source was read:
- (full) = page text read, through an extraction tool.
- (abstract) = abstract only, from a Europe PMC record.
- (snippet) = search-result summary only. Treat as weaker and check before relying on it.

Access problems: PMC, PubMed, the Royal Society, the University of Turku and iris.unil.ch blocked automated reading for several key papers. ameisenwiki.de failed with a certificate error, and ameisenforum.de, AntForum.org and AntWiki were not read. r/antkeeping appeared only as mirror-site snippets. Keeper evidence is therefore almost entirely Formiculture and antstore, and is mostly about other species.

## 1. Mould in test tubes and nests

### Takeaway
Keepers mostly treat mould as a warning sign rather than a killer. Reports where mould clearly killed a queen are few and confounded, because mould also grows on bodies that are already dead. No controlled study of ordinary tube mould in L. niger was found. The closest controlled evidence uses deliberately added pathogens.

### Cited Findings
- [keeper] Black mould on the cotton is widely called routine. Posters report no deaths from it, even with cotton fully covered, and say moving at every trace would mean moving constantly (snippet) — [Formiculture: mold in test tube](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/8299-there-is-mold-in-my-testube/), [moving from a moldy tube](https://formiculture.com/topic/7924-moving-ants-from-a-moldy-test-tube)
- [keeper] A supplier separates types: yellow mould signals unsanitary conditions, pink in cotton or water suggests bacteria, and ordinary mould justifies a new tube only if it spreads a lot or breaks down the cotton plug (snippet; vendor colour rules, no evidence given) — [Ant Gear](https://antgear.com/essential-tips-for-moving-your-colony-into-a-new-test-tube-setup/)
- [keeper] Usual triggers for a new tube: water running out, cotton covered or failing, worker die-off, discoloured water. The method is to connect a clean tube, darken it, and let ants move in their own time, usually overnight to a few days (snippet) — [Ant Gear](https://antgear.com/essential-tips-for-moving-your-colony-into-a-new-test-tube-setup/), [Formiculture: tips for long-lasting tubes](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/18765-tips-for-long-lasting-test-tube-setups/)
- [keeper, L. niger] In one colony the workers themselves carried the queen to an attached fresh tube when the old one moulded. She wandered, was found on her back, and died about two days later, and workers ate her head. The keeper suspected stress, and a reply said queens sometimes just die (full) — [Formiculture 17873](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17873-the-queen-is-dead-long-live-the-queen/)
- [keeper] Reported mould losses: seven Pheidole queens died of mould while the keeper was away, and a Polyrhachis queen died within an hour of capture with fungus on her (full, first page only) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] In another thread a member argued the queen died first and mould grew on the body afterwards; baking substrate was advised (snippet) — [Formiculture 18917](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/18917-why-has-my-queen-passed/)
- [keeper] Mould was seen growing on wet or sugary items in one classroom colony, species not named on the page read (full, page 2 only) — [Formiculture 12625](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/12625-orphan-colony-journal/page-2)
- [Ln] In ant-free plaster nests, fungus-like fruiting bodies began growing on old faecal patches. This was informal and unquantified. The paper reports no mould in occupied nests, and the authors suggest ants may suppress microbes, untested (full) — [Czaczkes et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4332866)
- [Ln] Founding queens in 15 ml tubes at 22 °C, n=50 per treatment. Mortality to day 35: sterile 10%, autoclaved soil 4%, untreated soil 6%, soil with added Metarhizium anisopliae and Serratia marcescens 16%. Mortality to the end of hibernation (day 221-225): 46%, 34%, 46%, 62%. Only the added-pathogen group differed significantly (P=0.021). Ordinary untreated soil was no worse than sterile (full) — [Front. Ecol. Evol. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/full)

### Inferences
- [inference] A fair model has mould as a slow signal of dirt and damp that raises risk, not a direct killer. Ordinary microbes barely changed queen survival in the 2020 study, and only added pathogens did.
- [inference] A body covered in mould says little about cause of death. A dead queen or ant that the workers cannot remove is the obvious food source.
- [inference] The most consistent keeper-side danger is the failed tube (cotton collapse, water gone, or a forced move), not the mould itself.

### Gaps
- No study was found that identifies which moulds grow in keeper tubes, or that measures mortality from them.
- No time course from first mould to death was found. Any number used in a game would be invented.
- The supplier's yellow and pink rules could not be checked against any source.

## 2. Mites, flies and pathogens

### Takeaway
Most mites keepers see are scavengers or hitch-hikers, and real parasitic mites are reported as rare but serious, usually arriving with collected ants. Metarhizium is the well-measured fungal killer of L. niger. Rickia wasmannii is not a Lasius problem, based on the sources found. Phorid flies were not documented as a captive threat in any source found.

### Cited Findings
**Mites**
- [keeper] Detritivorous mites on dead feeder insects are considered harmless. No ID was made in the thread. The advice is to remove dead insects and keep the foraging area dry and ventilated (full) — [Formiculture 3088](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/3088-mites-in-formicarium-dangerous-or-not)
- [keeper] In the same thread, parasitic mites are described as highly specialised and uncommon in captive colonies, most mites found on ants are described as phoretic, and a vendor says no known remedy exists for parasitised ants (full) — [Formiculture 3088](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/3088-mites-in-formicarium-dangerous-or-not)
- [keeper] Grain mites do not attack ants but keep coming back and are a nuisance. They can overrun feeder cultures such as fruit flies. Baking oats kills hitch-hikers (snippet) — [how bad are grain mites](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16898-how-bad-are-grain-mites), [feeding grain mites](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/15800-feeding-grain-mites-to-ants)
- [keeper] Reported control results are mixed. Hypoaspis predatory mites cleared one infestation, but another keeper says they fail on tiny white grain mites (snippet) — [predatory mites thread](https://formiculture.com/topic/13899-are-predatory-mites-legit/page-2)
- [keeper] Reported kills: three colonies were lost after an unchecked Prenolepis colony was added, and one Camponotus albosparsus colony was lost. Mite identity was not established (full, first page) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper, Lasius] Red ball-like mites were seen on the hind legs of two workers in a large Lasius host colony, unidentified, with no death reported. Rice and a water dip were suggested as untested remedies, and the dip was called stressful (full) — [Formiculture 10739](https://formiculture.com/topic/10739-blood-sucking-mites)

**Phorid and scuttle flies**
- [relative, secondary] An educational page reports Pseudacteon formicarum on Lasius, attacking workers only, with egg and larval stages of about 10 days and adults emerging 3-4 weeks later (snippet) — [UC Riverside](https://faculty.ucr.edu/~legneref/identify/phorid.htm). URL normalised from a malformed search result and not opened, so check it.
- [keeper] One forum post suggests P. formicarum for a fly attacking European Lasius, unconfirmed (snippet) — [antstore](https://www.antstore.net/forum/post66597.html)
- [relative] General scuttle flies breed in decaying plant and animal matter, and control means removing the breeding source (snippet; not ant-specific) — [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/phorid-flies)

**Fungi and other pathogens**
- [Ln] Metarhizium brunneum at 1.75×10⁸ spores/ml on founding queens caused strong mortality. Only one exposed nest produced any worker, against 85.5% of control nests. Queen number gave no protection (full; percentages only in a figure) — [Sci. Rep. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701228). This dose was applied deliberately and tells us nothing about natural exposure.
- [Ln, secondary] L. niger queens show immune priming against Beauveria bassiana, according to a later paper citing the original (snippet; original not read) — [PMC6780858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6780858)
- [relative] Rickia wasmannii is an obligate parasite of Myrmica. It shortened Myrmica scabrinodis lifespan over 2 months in lab nests (snippet) — [Csata et al. 2014](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00040-014-0349-3). Infected workers also need more water (snippet) — [Báthori et al. 2015 record](https://real.mtak.hu/28384/); this link came from the search list and its match to this paper was not confirmed.
- [relative] Host records in sources read are Myrmica species plus inquilines inside Myrmica nests. None is Lasius (snippet) — [Parasite 2016, PMC5112767](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5112767/)

### Inferences
- [inference] For L. niger keepers, Rickia should be treated as not applicable. No record on Lasius was found, though this is absence in the sources read, not proof.
- [inference] A game could separate scavenger mites (a cleanliness signal, harmless) from parasitic mites (rare, imported with new ants, hard to treat).

### Gaps
- Antennophorus, Aenigmatias and Gaeolaelaps: no source was retrieved, so nothing is claimed.
- No Aspergillus or Beauveria mortality curve in L. niger workers was found.
- A snippet mentioned mites killing a whole colony "within a day", but I could not tie it to a specific thread. Treat it as unverified.
- No keeper report of phorid flies killing a captive colony was found. This may reflect limited search, not absence.

## 3. L. niger social immunity

### Takeaway
L. niger is the species for network and faeces findings. Most detailed disinfection and grooming data come from L. neglectus, though destructive disinfection has also been measured in L. niger. Founding queens alone, or in groups, get almost none of this protection.

### Cited Findings
- [Ln] Stroeymeyt et al. 2018, Science, DOI 10.1126/science.aat4793. After pathogen exposure, both exposed ants and their nestmates changed behaviour in ways that reinforced contact-network properties limiting spread (abstract) — [Europe PMC record query](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:30467168&format=json&resultType=core), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30467168/)
- [Ln] Press release: automated tracking of 22 colonies over 24 hours using Metarhizium brunneum. Exposed and unexposed workers spent more time outside, and indoor workers moved to avoid contact, reducing risk to queen and young workers. No effect sizes given (full; press release) — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/778188)
- [Ln] Czaczkes et al. 2015: all 21 plaster nests (150-300 workers, 2 months) formed 1-4 faecal patches, mean 2.32, 41 in total, with 34 of 41 in chamber corners. They matched sugar dye colour and never contained solids. Food waste and corpses went to outside piles in all 21 colonies, and 3 colonies also had an outside sugar-coloured patch. Defecation was not directly observed, and health and pathogens were not measured (full) — [PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4332866)
- [Ln] Destructive disinfection in L. niger: 2 workers with 1 pupa, 20 replicates per treatment, from one colony, observed for 10 days. 95% of pathogen-exposed pupae were destroyed against 0% of controls (full, Materials and methods) — [Pull et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/32073)
- [relative: L. neglectus] Pupae are unpacked 2-10 days after exposure, sooner at higher doses, and are alive when unpacked. Spraying was about 13 times/day after unpacking against about once every 28 hours during sanitary care. With 8 ants per pupa, sporulation fell 65% after 1 day and 95% after 5 days. Sporulating pupae gave ants lethal infections, 42% of which became contagious after death, while disinfected pupae caused no transmission (full) — [Pull et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/32073)
- [relative: garden ants, L. neglectus per the group's work] Grooming of exposed brood includes oral uptake and spreading of poison, with formic acid as the main active part (abstract) — [Tragust et al. 2013, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.11.034)
- [relative: L. neglectus] Contact with exposed nestmates gave later survival protection. Infected workers kept away from brood, while healthy nestmates increased brood care (abstract) — [Ugelvig & Cremer 2007, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2007.10.029)
- [relative: Lasius] Grooming passed fungus to nestmates and caused low-level infections that rarely killed but raised antifungal ability and immune genes. No passive transfer of antimicrobials was found (snippet of abstract) — [Konrad et al. 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article/info:doi/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001300)
- [relative] Press coverage says exposed ants groomed themselves more and groomed healthy group members much less (snippet; press) — [phys.org 2015](https://phys.org/news/2015-04-infectious-ants-antisocial.pdf)
- [Ln] Co-founding queens barely interact. Allo-grooming occurred in 12 of 17,004 scans (0.07%), and self-grooming in 998 of 19,769 (5%), rising after puncture and fungal exposure (full) — [Sci. Rep. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701228)
- [Ln] Lone founding queens' formic acid use peaked in week 2 at sterile 10%, autoclaved 7%, untreated 9%, added-pathogen 19% of queens, with no significant treatment effect (P=0.458), then declined (full) — [Front. Ecol. Evol. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/full)
- [Ln] Workers keep dead out of the nest: dead workers went to waste piles, which made worker mortality hard to track, in a 64-week study (full) — [Schläppi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7320190/)
- [Ln, secondary] Queen pheromone production falls after immune challenge, and workers are more likely to execute surplus queens with low amounts (snippet of review) — [Holman 2010 record](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/2722852/queen-pheromones-the-chemical-crown-governing-insect-social-life)

### Inferences
- [inference] The founding stage is the undefended window, since a lone queen has only self-grooming and some formic acid, until the first workers appear.
- [inference] Colony-level defences should scale with worker numbers. The L. neglectus data (8 ants per pupa beat 3 at 1 day) points this way, but this is not measured for whole colonies.
- [inference] Faeces inside the nest in corners and solid waste outside is the best-supported layout rule. It comes from artificial plaster nests, so natural-soil behaviour is not established.

### Gaps
- Stroeymeyt effect sizes (workers per colony, percentage exposed, load and mortality results) could not be read. The paper is paywalled, and open copies were blocked or returned empty. I have left these out rather than recall them from memory.
- Corpse-removal rates and timing in L. niger were not retrieved.
- Tragust 2013 and Ugelvig & Cremer 2007 were abstract-level only. Tragust's and Ugelvig's species labels are based on secondary coverage and related papers.

## 4. Queen-related failure

### Takeaway
Founding in good lab conditions usually succeeds, with about 66-85% of nests producing workers. Losses climb sharply across the first winter in one study but not another. Workers become fertile without a queen, and queenless workers can live up to about three years, so an orphan colony can decline slowly rather than collapse.

### Cited Findings
- [Ln] 85.5% of control nests produced workers, on days 43-51 (mean day 45), mean 14.6 ± 2.1 workers from single queens by day 81 (full) — [Sci. Rep. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701228)
- [Ln] 66-84% of queens produced workers by day 83, with no significant treatment effect (P=0.175). Workers started eclosing in week 4 of a separate experiment (full) — [Front. Ecol. Evol. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/full). The week-4 figure conflicts with the mean of day 45 above, and the starting points may differ.
- [Ln] 18% of foundings are reported as pleometrotic (snippet of preprint) — [bioRxiv 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.16.603683.full.pdf). Nest output rose with queen number but per-queen output fell: 14.6, 9.3, 6.6 workers per queen for 1, 2, 4 queens (full) — [Sci. Rep. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701228)
- [keeper] Co-founding queens fight to the death once first workers hatch, and queens eat a few of their own eggs during founding (snippet; hobby site) — [antnest.co.uk](https://www.antnest.co.uk/?p=710). Wikipedia says the same on egg-eating but with a citation-needed tag (snippet) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_garden_ant). No primary evidence was found.
- [keeper] Eggs that never became larvae for weeks were suspected to be eaten (snippet; anecdote; link match not confirmed) — [Formiculture 8100](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/8100-lasius-neoniger/)
- [keeper] A queen "dead" and discarded was found to be alive (full, thread page) — [Formiculture 1030](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/1030-whats-the-dumbest-mistake-youve-made-in-this-hobby/page-3)
- [Ln] Orphaned workers develop large ovaries, and glass queen models coated in synthetic 3-methylhentriacontane kept them infertile. The compound also dominates the chemical profile of queen and eggs, and reduces aggression towards objects bearing it (snippet of press and record; DOI lookup returned no record) — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/663856), [Holman et al. 2010 record](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/2722871/identification-of-an-ant-queen-pheromone-regulating-worker-steril)
- [relative, review] Orphaned workers commonly lay unfertilised eggs that become males (snippet) — [Psyche, Cambridge Entomological Club](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mac/projects/psyche/95/95-059.html)
- [Ln] Queenless cohorts at 22 °C without hibernation: mean worker lifespan 434 days (95% CI ±44, early-stage colonies) and 309 days (±36, intermediate colonies), maximum 1129 and 1094 days (full PDF text) — [Kramer et al. 2016](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/publications/_pdf/Kramer_etal_ExpGerontol_2016.pdf)
- [Ln] Queens up to 28 years, average workers one to two years (Kramer citing Kutter & Stumper 1969) — [Kramer et al. 2016](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/publications/_pdf/Kramer_etal_ExpGerontol_2016.pdf). Conflict: a compendium attributes 7-8 years for workers to the same old source (snippet, unverified) — [UF Book of Insect Records](https://entnemdept.ufl.edu/walker/ufbir/chapters/chapter_34.shtml/index_order.shtml)
- [keeper] Options after queen death: care for workers until they die, freeze them, or try a parasitic queen such as Lasius umbratus. Keepers' reports of Lasius umbratus adoption were not read (full; snippet) — [Formiculture 17873](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17873-the-queen-is-dead-long-live-the-queen/), [antstore](https://www.antstore.net/forum/post148101.html)
- [keeper] L. niger will quickly kill any foreign ant, queen or worker (snippet; hobby page) — [antnest.co.uk](https://www.antnest.co.uk/?p=3011)
- [relative] Monogyne Formica selysi workers were significantly more aggressive to foreign queens of monogyne origin (snippet) — [Anim. Behav. record](https://isidore.science/document/10.1016/j.anbehav.2006.11.028). Link match not confirmed.

### Inferences
- [inference] These cohorts had no queen, so they give a plausible upper guide to orphan colony lifespan: about half to one year mean, up to about three years. Hibernation or colony-level care may shift this.
- [inference] Orphan worker egg-laying should yield only males, at low productivity.

### Gaps
- No L. niger timeline was found for when orphan workers start laying, how many males result, or whole-colony decline. Timing data retrieved are from other species.
- Sommer & Hölldobler's work on L. niger queen reduction was not retrieved. The bioRxiv fighting threshold was not read.
- No frequency for infertile queens or first-brood failure beyond the worker-production rates above. A queen with no brood may have several causes.
- Worker aggression to an introduced L. niger queen was not measured in any source read. A Formiculture thread on adopted-queen attacks was blocked ([18848](https://formiculture.com/topic/18848-help-workers-attacking-adopted-queen)).

## 5. Environmental failure

### Takeaway
Heat and water mistakes dominate the keeper stories, and they happen fast. Measured cold limits are far below normal hibernation temperatures, so cold deaths are probably not about simple freezing. Overwintering temperature does matter for workers.

### Cited Findings
**Heat**
- [keeper] Reported heat losses: a ~50-worker Camponotus colony died in the sun; a Formica queen overheated outdoors (full) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] A colony left on a wax melter died with the nest charred; a queen on a hot outdoor table died; overheated test tubes flooded, killing 2 workers and a queen; a knocked-over heat source broke an outworld and colonies died (full) — [Formiculture 1030](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/1030-whats-the-dumbest-mistake-youve-made-in-this-hobby/page-3)
- [keeper] Guide advice is to heat the nest side, not the water side (snippet; page blocked) — [AntOnTop](https://antontop.com/why-ants-die/)

**Water**
- [keeper] A Camponotus irritans colony lost most workers after three days without water, and a C. novaeboracensis queen died kept without water (full) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] "A few" colonies were killed or nearly killed by forgotten watering. One colony died from flooding through a watering hole in an extension (full) — [Formiculture 1030](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/1030-whats-the-dumbest-mistake-youve-made-in-this-hobby/page-3)
- [Ln] Warm acclimation raised drought resistance and increased solid cuticular hydrocarbons in L. niger (snippet) — [J. Exp. Biol. 2022 record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/62c0230b5aee126c0f0d44f2/why-do-ants-differ-in-acclimatory-ability-biophysical-mechanisms-behind-cuticular-hydrocarbon)

**Cold and hibernation**
- [Ln] Workers' LT50 about -13 to -15 °C and supercooling about -24 to -25 °C, more than enough for the coldest areas studied (snippet) — [Contemp. Probl. Ecol. 2021](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1408474629733666816)
- [Ln] 280 founding queens: wintering at +2 °C versus +7 to +8 °C did not change queen survival, and +2 °C queens kept more fat. In 1-year-old colonies, +2 °C lowered worker survival. Queen survival differed between years (snippet; publisher and repository blocked) — [Haatanen et al. 2015 record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3223334?lang=en_US)
- [Ln] Hibernation at 4 °C for 5 months: queen mortality rose from 4-16% at day 35 to 34-62% by the end of hibernation. Period includes everything from day 35 to days 221-225, not hibernation alone (full) — [Front. Ecol. Evol. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/full)
- [Ln] Contrast: at 6 °C from week 15 to 32 with 2 weeks at 14 °C each side, no queen died during hibernation. Over 64 weeks 6 of 30 died (20%) (full) — [Schläppi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7320190/)
- [keeper] A vendor lists hibernation at 6 °C, required for the species (snippet) — [Formica Market](https://formicamarket.com/hi/species/lasius-niger)

### Inferences
- [inference] The very different winter figures (34-62% queen loss to end of hibernation, against none during hibernation) probably reflect different conditions. Possible factors are 4 versus 6 °C, abrupt versus stepped cooling, and tube and food conditions. Neither study tested this and sample sizes differ, so it is a hypothesis.
- [inference] Dehydration and overheating act in hours to days in these reports, far faster than mould or mites.

### Gaps
- No CTmax or CTmin for L. niger, no heat-mat incident data, and no drowning time courses were found.
- A forum claim that 28 °C is "absolutely too hot" for L. niger appeared in a snippet, and I could not tie it to a thread or test it. Do not use it as a limit.
- Colony-level hibernation loss percentages from Haatanen et al. were not readable.

## 6. Food-related failure

### Takeaway
Too much protein with no alternative is a well-demonstrated killer of L. niger workers, but ants given a free choice avoid most of the harm. Sublethal insecticide hits slowly: colonies looked normal for months and fell behind only later. Sticky-food drowning and spoiled food are keeper-level reports.

### Cited Findings
- [Ln] Dussutour & Simpson 2012 (Proc. R. Soc. B, DOI 10.1098/rspb.2012.0051): high-protein low-carbohydrate diets shortened worker lifespan by up to 10-fold, mainly due to excess protein rather than lack of carbohydrate. One day on a high-protein diet reduced population size by over 20% (abstract) — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1098/rspb.2012.0051&format=json&resultType=core), [PMC3350685](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3350685)
- [Ln] CNRS press note on that paper: 32 colonies per experiment. High-sugar diets gave more than a year, and high-protein diets gave at most a month or two with sharp decline after about 15 days. Colonies given sugar and protein fractions in separate dishes lived almost as long as on high-sugar (about 1 year), and protein-only colonies under a month. Across 40 colonies, one day of high protein cut lifespan by 10-20% (full; press note, species not named in it) — [CNRS note copy](https://www.dictionnaire-amoureux-des-fourmis.fr/P/Presse/2012/Alimentation%20fourmis%20Dussutour.docx). The 10-20% and "over 20%" figures differ, and may use different measures.
- [relative: Argentine ants, not L. niger] Arganda et al. 2017: free amino acids shortened lifespan more than whole protein, methionine, serine, threonine and phenylalanine were especially harmful, and an exome-matched mix did not change longevity (abstract; species from search summary) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.2052), [PMC5247493](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5247493). **The brief assumed Arganda used L. niger, but the record found indicates Argentine ants. The L. niger study is Dussutour & Simpson 2012.**
- [Ln] Schläppi et al. 2020: thiamethoxam in sugar water at 4.5 and 30 µg/L nominal, n=10 per group. At week 13 there was no significant difference. At week 64, median workers were 290.5 control, 188 low, 151.5 high, and median larvae 105.5, 49, 35.5 (larvae significant only at high dose). Queen survival was unaffected, 8/10 in each group. Long-term exposure and small sample (full) — [PMC7320190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7320190/). The paper's stated "38% and 56% larger" controls do not match percentages from medians (my calculation: 35% and 48% fewer).
- [keeper] A Dorymyrmex queen drowned in a honey and sugar-water mix (full) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] A colony (extraction says "Tetra", probably Tetramorium) died about a month after being fed an outdoor insect. The cause was not established (full) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] A commenter says mass die-offs usually indicate poisoning (snippet; opinion) — [Formiculture 18105](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/18105-repeated-mass-deaths-in-colonies-similar-experiences-or-solutions). Link match not confirmed.
- [keeper] Keepers ask about pesticide-contaminated food when diagnosing a L. niger queen death (full) — [Formiculture 17873](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17873-the-queen-is-dead-long-live-the-queen/)

### Inferences
- [inference] A protein penalty in a game should apply when sugar is missing or protein is forced into a mixed food, not simply when protein is offered, since a separate-dish choice removed most of the harm.
- [inference] Contaminated wild-caught feeder risk could be slow and hard to attribute. This extends Schläppi's chronic dosing to a single contaminated insect, which was not tested.

### Gaps
- Exact P:C ratios and median lifespans per diet in Dussutour & Simpson 2012 could not be read because the full text was blocked. The role of larvae was also not read.
- No acute toxicity data for contaminated feeder insects in L. niger, and no frequency of sticky-food drowning.
- A related paper (Front. Ecol. Evol. 2022, protein-rich diet in fertile workers) was not read, and its species was not checked: [PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2022.1098245/pdf).

## 7. Escapes, handling damage, light and vibration

### Takeaway
Handling accidents kill individual queens directly and are commonly reported. The claim that light or vibration stress harms Lasius rests on keeper belief and on relocation research in other species. No controlled Lasius test was found.

### Cited Findings
- [keeper] Reported handling losses: queen squished, queen crushed by a slammed lid, queen trapped in a gap, queen cut by a tube connector (died next day), queen killed by aspirator mishap, dropped and broken tube with colony dying "from stress" (full) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/), [Formiculture 1030](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/1030-whats-the-dumbest-mistake-youve-made-in-this-hobby/page-3)
- [keeper] Escapes reported in the same pages: 1 escape; a queen kept in a tank never seen again (full) — same threads.
- [keeper] Keepers ask how often a colony is checked as a possible stress factor (full) — [Formiculture 17873](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17873-the-queen-is-dead-long-live-the-queen/)
- [keeper] A guide claims stress makes workers abandon brood, and another warns that forcing ants across with tweezers stops egg-laying for weeks (snippet; exact page not confirmed) — [AntOnTop](https://antontop.com/?p=22081)
- [keeper] Opposing view: a commenter says keeping colonies covered causes more long-run stress, because the colony becomes used to darkness (snippet; mirror) — [r/antkeeping mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/antkeeping/comments/1idmphr/people_who_broke_some_of_the_rules_of_keeping)
- [keeper, L. niger] Forced moving caused no obvious stress for one colony. Another report calls L. niger very loyal to its nest, with about 80% moved in about three weeks (snippet; which thread holds which claim not confirmed) — [Formiculture 21181](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/21181-encourange-lasius-niger-to-move-colony/), [17577](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17577-question-moving-ants-out-of-old-test-tube/)
- [relative] Leptothorax colonies quickly react to light in brood chambers and move all members to a darker site. Colonies without brood are much less likely to move in a Lasius flavus and Myrmica rubra study, and moving costs energy (snippet) — [The Biologist](https://thebiologist.rsb.org.uk/biologist-features/why-do-ants-move-house-2)

### Inferences
- [inference] Light and vibration may act mainly by triggering brood relocation and wasted effort, rather than by direct harm. The evidence for L. niger is indirect.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of light or vibration stress in Lasius was found.
- No data on how often or how long escapes persist, or on how escaped queens fare.

## 8. Rough frequency and full failure catalogue

### Takeaway
No survey of captive colony deaths was found. The only available signal is self-reported forum tallies, which suggest that everyday husbandry errors (heat, water, handling) are the largest named causes, with a large "unexplained" share. Mites and mould are reported but less often. This is weak evidence, biased towards memorable stories, and mostly not about L. niger.

### Cited Findings
- [keeper] "Worst deaths" thread, first page of three, 15 losses in mixed species: unexplained or missing 3, mould 2, mites 2, overheating 2, crushing 2, dehydration 2, drowning 1, trapped 1, escape 0 (full; tallied by extraction tool, spot-check before quoting) — [Formiculture 16438](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16438-worst-deaths-from-colonysqueens/)
- [keeper] "Dumbest mistake" thread, posts 41-60: heat at least 3 cases plus a heat-source accident, flooding 2, underwatering "a few" colonies, handling and breakage at least 3, DIY setup failures "numerous", escapes or releases 3. No pesticide cases on this page (full; same caveat) — [Formiculture 1030](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/1030-whats-the-dumbest-mistake-youve-made-in-this-hobby/page-3)
- [Ln] In controlled settings, background founding loss was 4-16% by day 35 and 20% of queens across 64 weeks with care — [Front. Ecol. Evol. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076/full), [Schläppi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7320190/)

### Inferences
- [inference] Rough ranking from forum signal: husbandry errors (heat, water, handling), then unexplained, then parasites and mould, then food poisoning. Treat this as ordering only, not as rates.

### Gaps

- German forums and AntWiki were not usable in this session, so L. niger-specific keeper frequency remains unknown.
