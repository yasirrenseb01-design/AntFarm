## What do keepers actually use?

### Takeaway
I found no poll, survey or sales data that ranks nest types, so any ranking is an impression. The best-supported impression is that the test tube is the near-universal first home. After that the market is split between acrylic, gypsum or Ytong, and 3D-printed nests, with no measured leader.

### Cited Findings
- **Survey evidence:** the closest thing found is an informal 2016 class questionnaire on Formiculture. About 12 people answered: 8 make their nests and 4 buy. It asked nothing about nest type, is self-selected and falls outside the 2024-2026 window [survey, very weak] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/3951-questionnaire/)
- **Test tube first:** beginner advice in English, Dutch-shop and Thai sources all starts with a test tube [ผู้เลี้ยง-consensus; basis: agreement across the sources below, no counts]:
  - [Quality Ants guide](https://qualityants.nl/en/purchase-formicarium/) [vendor] [F]
  - [Pantip beginner post](https://pantip.com/topic/37149956) [single keeper] [F]
  - [ALTV/Thai PBS](https://altv.thaipbs.or.th/content/pr/615339f6f3a5e8670b38a4d3) calls it cheap and widely favoured [S; the page was too large to fetch]
- **Forum recommendations by seller:** in a 2021 "best non-DIY" thread, 5 posts named one US maker of premium small nests. Another maker of wood nests got 3 mentions, and 10 other sellers got 1-2 each. One post warned against 3D-printed sellers. These are mention counts from page 1 of 2 with 27 replies, not usage data [ผู้เลี้ยง-consensus, small sample, outside the date window] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16895-best-non-diy-formicariums/)
- **One keeper's actual setup:** in that thread, one keeper housed 4 of 6 colonies in nests from a small Etsy maker [single keeper] [F] — [same thread](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16895-best-non-diy-formicariums/)
- **Popularity claims by material**, all unquantified and from sellers:
  - Acrylic "everywhere", Ytong a popular DIY material, and 3D printing "changed the hobby" [vendor; the seller makes printed nests] [F] — [Antastic Tom](https://antastictom.com/pages/formicarium-materials-compared)
  - Ytong popular "for years" [vendor] [F] — [Quality Ants](https://qualityants.nl/en/purchase-formicarium/)
- **Best-seller lists:**
  - A German shop's "Best Selling" panel showed only small parts: mesh inserts at 3.90-5.90 EUR and a hose connector at 4.90 EUR [vendor] [F] — [antcube.shop](https://antcube.shop/en/product/ytongnest-40x20-with-arena/). This tells us little about nests, since cheap parts sell in volume.
  - No other vendor's best-seller list was retrievable.
- **Review counts as a weak demand signal:** a Swedish shop's small wood-plastic modular nest shows 220 reviews out of 766 for the shop, averaging 4.8/5. Reviews are hosted by the vendor and grouped across related products [vendor] [F] — [Antkeepers](https://antkeepers.com/products/nest-formicarium-ant-farm-v2)
- **Material trade-offs**, where sources broadly agree [vendor, two sellers]:
  - Acrylic gives good visibility but no moisture of its own.
  - Ytong and gypsum hold moisture but stain and are harder to clean.
  - Printed PLA is precise and modular but is not a good moisture medium, and its layer lines trap dirt.
  - Soil gives natural digging but poor viewing.
  - [F] — [Antastic Tom](https://antastictom.com/pages/formicarium-materials-compared); [Quality Ants](https://qualityants.nl/en/purchase-formicarium/)
- **No best material:** both guides say no single material suits every species [vendor] [F] (same links).

### Inferences
- Impression order for first homes: test tube, far ahead of everything else. This is reasonably safe.
- Impression for second homes: acrylic-fronted and printed nests dominate what is sold ready-made, based on catalogues I read, not on purchases. Ytong, plaster and soil look more like DIY or European-shop options.
- Natural or bioactive looks like a display niche rather than a default. I found sellers' guides but no keeper counts.

### Gaps
- No usage poll for 2024-2026. Colony-journal counts were not attempted; they would need manual sampling of Formiculture journals.
- r/antkeeping is missing entirely. It may well lean differently from Formiculture.

## How does this differ by region, including Thailand?

### Takeaway
The clearest regional effect is legal. The US requires a federal permit to move any ant species across state lines, so its keepers lean towards local catches and selling equipment without ants. European shops openly sell colony-plus-nest bundles. Thailand has a real but thinly documented hobby, with cheap marketplace imports and DIY from local materials.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- The federal plant-health agency regulates import and interstate movement of all ant species, and a species- and state-specific permit is needed. Buying is legal only if the supplier holds a valid permit. Catching local ants is called the best practice, and release is prohibited [official] [S] — [USDA APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/organism-soil-imports/invertebrate-pets)
- A marketplace summary of the rules gives about 127 days' average processing and advises applying 280 days ahead. It is secondary and carries its own disclaimer [vendor/secondary] [F] — [FormicaMarket](https://formicamarket.com/sl/species/lasius-niger/legality/us)
- A forum claim that only queens are restricted conflicts with the APHIS text, so trust APHIS [single keeper, contradicted] [S] — [Formiculture](https://formiculture.com/topic/8007-is-it-legal-to-ship-ants-within-state-borders)

**Germany and the EU**
- A German shop sells complete colony-plus-habitat bundles, such as colony, arena and accessories for 149.90 EUR [vendor] [F] — [antcube.shop](https://antcube.shop/product/bundle-offer-lasius-niger). It is run by the Berlin firm behind [antstore.net](https://antcube.shop/en/product/ytongnest-40x20-with-arena/) [F].
- That shop also lists 35 starter-set items, with subcategories for habitat, dig-in plate, cork, gypsum, ants and combinations [vendor] [F] — [antstore.net](https://www.antstore.net/shop/de/antfarms---nests/acrylglas-formicaria/acrylic-glass-plate-antfarms/ANTCUBE-PF-20-M-----Lab-5426.html)
- The same shop's "popular species" category leads with *Lasius niger* and *Messor barbarus* [vendor] [F] (same link).
- Ytong is treated as a mainstream ready-made product there, not only DIY [vendor] [F] — [antcube.shop](https://antcube.shop/en/product/ytongnest-40x20-with-arena/)
- The German-law claim I found (no restriction on trade) was an old forum post with no official backing, so treat it as unverified [single keeper] [S] — search set included [Formiculture 5301](https://formiculture.com/topic/5301-ant-theft-in-germany) and [Formiculture 5561](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/5561-collecting-queens-and-colonies-in-europe/). The exact post was not confirmed.

**United Kingdom**
- Live-queen listings are cheap and common, starting from about 9 GBP (see the price tables below) [vendor] [S].
- Most exotic species are described as purchasable, subject to local law [S, Wikipedia] — [Ant-keeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-keeping)
- An old UK retailer post says imports without border inspection and certificates are illegal [vendor, old] [S] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/13690-laws-regarding-import-into-the-uk-and-eu/)
- One UK seller states release of a sold species is illegal [vendor] [S] — [Ant Antics](https://www.antantics.wales/product-page/polyrhachis-cf-cyaniventris-blueberry-ant)

**Asia outside Thailand**
- A 2021 report places keepers mainly in the US, EU, Australia and China, and says most are in high school or younger. No figures back this up [press] [F] — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/unlikely-rise-of-antkeeping)
- A 2020 wire report says one Singapore shop sold 30 species in starter kits at about 13.90-300 SGD, and that such farms are rare there [press] [S] — [Taipei Times/Reuters](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/world/archives/2020/09/15/2003743456)

- A report says over a quarter of species traded in China were non-native despite an import ban. I did not confirm which article carried this; the search set included [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-11-illegal-antics-tiny-targets-of-a-growing-global-biopiracy-threat-to-south-africa.md) and [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/one-ant-for-220-the-new-frontier-of-wildlife-trafficking/) [press] [S].

**Thailand**
- A Thai aggregator listing, updated 6 Nov 2026, shows a real marketplace on Lazada and Shopee: test tubes, acrylic nests, small printed nests and "castle" novelty boxes. Many titles look machine-translated. No gel products appeared on that page [marketplace listing] [F] — [Priceza](https://www.priceza.com/s/%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B2/%E0%B8%9A%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%9A%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%94)
- Thai press in April 2023 described ant keeping as a growing hobby, with enthusiasts selling replica nests and earning tens of thousands of baht a month. Only the headline and intro were readable, and the income figure is unverified [press] [F, partial] — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/local/2689736)
- The same article reportedly says beginners start in tubes and move to clear acrylic later when skilled [S] — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/local/2689736)
- A 2019 Thai DIY guide lists nests made from test tubes, sauce cups, rice boxes, clear cups, soil and plaster. Its author prefers plaster poured in a food box. It credits a Facebook keepers' group (กลุ่มคนเลี้ยงมด) and a Facebook page (AntKeepingThailand) [single keeper] [F] — [phungnim blog](https://phungnim.blogspot.com/2019/06/allantnet.html)
- A Pantip beginner post uses a tube, cotton, a small plastic box as the outworld and only Thai-named local species (มดตะลาน recommended first). It gives no costs [single keeper, undated] [F] — [Pantip](https://pantip.com/topic/37149956)
- Imported kits also appear on cross-border sites, for example a US sand-and-rock kit listed in KWD on a Thai page [marketplace] [S] — [Ubuy TH](https://www.ubuy.co.th/th/product/2CTSAUQY-sand-ant-farm-big-anthill-formicarium-educational-ants)

### Inferences
- US: law favours keepers who catch local queens and buy nests only. This fits the many nest-only US listings, but that link is my reasoning, not a sourced finding.
- Thailand: guides centre on locally caught queens and home-made plaster, so a Thai player would recognise tube, rice box and plaster as authentic. Most Thai keeper text I found dates from 2012-2023.

### Gaps
- Thai import or trade law for ants: nothing found.
- UK and EU law for ants: no official source read. From memory, unchecked, some invasive ant species (*Solenopsis*, *Wasmannia*) are on the EU's invasive-species list; verify this before relying on it.
- No Thai shop specialising in ant keeping was identified by name, and the size of the Thai community is unknown. The Facebook groups would be the place to look.
- No Japan or Malaysia data.

## Beginner versus experienced: first purchase, switching, regrets, and gel farms

### Takeaway
The usual path is test tube, then a small founding nest, then expansion only when clearly needed. Experienced keepers seem to spread towards DIY, natural materials and nests matched to species. Gel farms are condemned consistently, and that is the strongest consensus in this research.

### Cited Findings
- **Staged advice:** start small and move up only when the tube is clearly too small, since oversized nests leave unused chambers that are prone to mold [vendor; advice against selling bigger, so arguably less self-serving] [F] — [Quality Ants](https://qualityants.nl/en/purchase-formicarium/)
- **A US maker's advice:** it also discourages oversizing and advises against its largest nests for a single queen [vendor] [F] — [Tar Heel Ants Q&A](https://tarheelants.com/pages/q-and-a)
- **How long a small nest lasts, sources conflict:**
  - The maker says about 25-30 workers, roughly 1-1.5 years for one *Camponotus* species [vendor] [F] — [Q&A](https://tarheelants.com/pages/q-and-a)
  - A forum user says colonies usually leave after a couple of months, calling it poor value on a tight budget [single keeper] [S] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/10194-thoughts-on-th-a-mini-hearth/)
- **DIY as a budget entry:** tube plus outworld for under 20 USD [affiliate review site] [F] — [AntAddict](https://antaddict.com/best-ant-farm/). A hobbyist quoted in 2021 reported spending at most 10 USD a month [single keeper] [F] — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/unlikely-rise-of-antkeeping)
- **Experienced preferences:** in the 2021 thread, one keeper prefers wood or natural materials and dislikes printed nests. Another reports mixed results with a sand-between-glass farm and cork nests, while liking glass outworlds [single keepers] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/16895-best-non-diy-formicariums/)
- **Gel farm complaints:**
  - A June 2024 thread names mold (3 posters), poor nutrition (3), light stress (2, disputed by one), plus humidity, cave-ins and being sold with queenless workers (1 each) [ผู้เลี้ยง-consensus, 4 posters] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/21358-gel-farms-are-really-bad-why-exactly/)
  - A 2022 thread on Amazon reviews reports ants that stop digging, gooey collapses and dead ants, while noting a five-star average. Cheap gel sets were about 20 USD; the suggested alternatives were a tube and cotton in a tub, or a real colony [ผู้เลี้ยง-consensus] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/18773-the-depressing-world-of-gel-farm-amazon-reviews/)
  - A 2026 review site agrees and says gel molds within weeks [affiliate] [F] — [AntAddict](https://antaddict.com/best-ant-farm/)
- **Sand-farm novelty kits:** sold for kids at about 13-25 USD, with workers only via a voucher [affiliate] [F] — [AntAddict](https://antaddict.com/best-ant-farm/)

### Inferences
- A realistic game could show gel or novelty kits as a tempting cheap trap: bright but with short-lived workers and no queen.
- The strongest "regret" pattern in my sources is buying too big or buying gel. Direct first-person regret posts were not found, so this is assembled from advice.

### Gaps
- No first-person "what I regret buying" threads were found (Reddit is the likely home for them).
- No specific independent critique of the AntsCanada Hybrid Nest was found. The only related item is one forum comment that the channel under-warns viewers about pitfalls [single keeper] [S] — [search set, thread not identified](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/12397-hello-from-south-carolina)

## Trends and who drives them

### Takeaway
Printed and modular nests are clearly visible in new shops, but keepers remain split on them. Bioactive setups are promoted more than they are measured.

### Cited Findings
- **Printed nests, keeper opinion:**
  - A 2022 thread split 2 critical, 3 favourable and 4 neutral. One keeper's colony deaths were mostly in bare printed nests and a grout coating fixed it; another says *Messor* chew weak plastic. ## Durability and resale

### Takeaway
Wear data is scarce and anecdotal. Barriers and consumables need regular renewal, while nests are usually replaced because the colony outgrows them, not because they wear out. I found no real resale data.

### Cited Findings
- **Barrier upkeep:** PTFE barrier should be reapplied as needed, at least every 2-3 months, and shaken every 2-3 weeks in storage [vendor] [F] — [Tar Heel Ants](https://tarheelants.com/products/fluon-1)
- **Barrier supply:** a 4 oz bottle lasted one keeper at least a year [single keeper, 2020] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14607-budget-fluon/)
- **Barrier alternatives (2020):** olive oil lasts months but fails when wet; powder with alcohol is messy and needs constant reapplication; Vaseline was rejected [single keepers] [F] — same thread
- **Colony outgrows the nest:** queens can live 10-20 years and outlast any nest; one nest size lasts about 1-2 years depending on species [vendor] [F] — [Q&A](https://tarheelants.com/pages/q-and-a)
- **Porous materials:** they stain with use, seen as normal, and thin gypsum chips. Printed PLA behaviour changes under long moisture and heat, and hybrid designs let parts be swapped [vendor] [F] — [Antastic Tom](https://antastictom.com/pages/formicarium-materials-compared)
- **Spare parts sold:** clips, screws and connectors are sold separately, which suggests these are expected to wear or get lost [vendor] [F] — [catalog](https://antastictom.com/products.json?limit=250)
- **Scratching:** a printed nest's top can scratch [single keeper] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19936-ant-shack/)
- **Sponges:** they mold more readily and small sponge chambers dry out fast [single keeper] [F] — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/19064-3d-printed-nests/)
- **Water deposits:** PLA pores may clog with hard-water deposits, cleared by monthly rinses and vinegar baths [vendor-style article] [S] — [FacFox](https://www.facfox3d.com/info/why-3d-printed-ant-farms-are-the-buzz-in-the-a-103000716.html)
- **Ytong hazard:** unwashed dust reportedly killed a colony overnight [single keeper] [S] — [antstore.net forum](https://www.antstore.net/forum/post142255.html). Which post in that set carried this was not confirmed.
- **Resale:** a Polish-registered marketplace exists for new and used nests, but its formicarium page showed zero listings when seen [F] — [FormicaMarket](https://formicamarket.com/fr/formicariums)

### Inferences
- For a game: model barriers, cotton, sponges and water as recurring upkeep, and treat nests as upgrades rather than items that break. Porous nests could gain visible staining.

### Gaps
- No measured lifespans or replacement intervals for any nest type.
- No resale price data. Keeper forums' for-sale sections were not sampled.
